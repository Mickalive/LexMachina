## 2. Fix 1 — mode-0.7 zoom baseline

**Problem:** the two sides of the comparison used different, non-inter-operable metric
conventions. `state/fractal-map.json` records the ACCEPTED N=1000 zoom rates as
**child-cluster-level** fractions (0.1944 = 28/144 for 0.5, 0.2759 = 40/145 for 0.7),
whereas the N=1200 build's `improvement_rate` is the **per-transition-average of
coarse-cluster improvement rates**. Comparing 0.265 against 0.2759 (or 0.234) mixes
conventions and is not a valid measure of improvement or decline.

**Resolution (honest, single convention):** we recomputed BOTH the N=1000 (accepted) and
N=1200 per-transition-average zoom improvement rate from the committed label arrays using
the **exact same** `compute_zoom_coherence` convention (fraction of coarse clusters,
size>=3, branch known, whose mean-child-purity improves on zoom, averaged over the 6
transitions). Result:

| mode | N=1000 (accepted, recomputed PTA) | N=1200 (PTA) | direction |
|------|-----------------------------------|--------------|-----------|
| 0.5  | 0.1802                            | 0.2500       | IMPROVED (+0.0698) |
| 0.7  | 0.2115                            | 0.2414       | IMPROVED (+0.0298) |

Under the single consistent convention **both modes improve** at N=1200. The false
"0.265 ABOVE accepted (0.234)" and "stable-to-improved" statements were removed. We also
explicitly document that the accepted-state 0.2759 uses the child-cluster-level convention
and is therefore not numerically comparable to the per-transition-average N=1200 value
(treating that forced comparison as "decline" would be a convention mismatch, not a real
decline).

Reference:
`results/fractal_map/evaluation/scale_readiness_independent_recompute_33317520019.json`
(section `zoom_improvement_recompute`).
