### D2 — counter labels are evidence-gated, guarded, and the drop is disclosed

`classify_against_intent` was a negation-blind, boilerplate-blind, topic-blind substring
match; 22/40 new rows carried unsupported labels (4 affirmatively inverted).

Repairs in `product/query/adversarial_retrieval.py` (`adversarial_retrieval/2`):

1. **Disposal/dismissal words leave the licensing lexicon** (`abgelehnt`, `abgewiesen`,
   `rejeté`, `écarté`, `respinto`, bare `entgegen`). They remain in the *probe* vocabulary,
   where they retrieve contrary discussion; they can no longer license a label, because on a
   real decision they usually mean the appeal failed and the challenged ruling stood.
   `DISPOSAL_SENTENCE_PATTERNS` additionally blocks any contrary label on a sentence that
   narrates a recours/Berufung/Beschwerde dismissal (incl. the verbatim audit examples).
2. **Eintretens / procedural formulas never license.** `BOILERPLATE_SENTENCE_PATTERNS`
   covers the BGer admissibility formula ("unter Vorbehalt der genügenden Begründung …
   einzutreten"), the "vereinfachten Verfahren als Ausnahme" construction and the
   Rechtsmissbrauchsvorbehalt.
3. **Topic gate.** A label is issued only when the matched sentence shares a term with
   `bundle_topic_terms(bundle)` = answer key terms + query tokens + the initial pool's
   shared regeste vocabulary (frequency ≥ max(3, n/12), bounded at 24 terms, recorded in
   `provenance.adversarial_retrieval.label_topic_terms`). The pool component is the
   **cross-lingual bridge**: Swiss case law is trilingual, an Italian answer's terms never
   appear in a German BGE abstract, yet the initial retrieval already established those
   decisions are on-topic. Without it the first repair re-run measured IT counter rows =
   0 (measured, preserved in this cycle's development log) — a monolingual topic gate is
   a worse product defect than the one it fixes.
4. **Sentence-scoped markers.** `counter_markers` now reports only the markers present in
   the cited evidence sentence, not the union over the decision text (audit example
   `bger_4A_82_2024`: `['ausgenommen','einschränkung','sauf','vorbehalt']` vs evidence
   containing only `vorbehalt` — now `['vorbehalt']`).
