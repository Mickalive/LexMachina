## 4. Specific Findings

### 4.1 Incoming Citations Are Structurally Non-Functional (Informational)
The citation graph stores outgoing references as external identifiers (e.g., "BGE 133 II 249", "1C_704/2020") that do not match the decision_id format (e.g., "bger_1C_704_2020"). As a result, the incoming citation index maps 1,628 external references, but none resolve to corpus decisions. The `get_incoming()` method works correctly but returns empty lists for all corpus decisions.

**Impact:** The `/api/citations` endpoint returns 0 incoming citations for all decisions. The frontend correctly handles this (displays "Cited by: (none)"). This is a design limitation, not a defect — the citation graph represents external legal references, not an internal citation network.

**Classification:** Informational. Not a blocking defect. The state does not claim incoming citations resolve to corpus decisions.

### 4.2 Section Mode Coverage Is Low (6.3%)
Only 63 of 1,000 map decisions have section-based projections. The state honestly documents this: "Section modes cover 63 of 1000 decisions (6.3%)."

**Impact:** When viewing a section mode, 63 decisions have section-specific positions and 937 show background positions from the main map. The section mode is functional but limited.

**Classification:** Honest limitation, documented in state and report.

### 4.3 Legal Area Purity Is Low Across All Section Modes
Section mode clustering purity for legal area ranges from 0.143 (dispositiv at resolution 0.5) to 0.413 (dispositiv at resolution 2.0). Language purity is higher (0.60–0.95).

**Impact:** Section modes cluster more by language than by legal area. This is honest negative evidence — the section-based representations do not yet produce strongly legally coherent clusters.

**Classification:** Honest negative result. First-class evidence for the legal-distance lane.
