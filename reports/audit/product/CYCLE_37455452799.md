### Jurist Preference Claims — Critical Examination

**Operational Resume Claim**: "Jurist Preference: **0.78-0.79** vs semantic baseline **0.43** — Satisfies mission: 'beating simple semantic-map baselines in legal usefulness'"

**Evidence Source** (`app/evaluation_loader.py:HOLDOUT_METRICS`):

```python
"cited_outcome_hybrid_0.5": {
    "jp_score": 0.7990,
    "jp_note": "train (no holdout available), PRODUCTION DEFAULT per v15b-audit",
    ...
},
"cited_outcome_hybrid_0.7": {
    "jp_score": 0.7907,
    "jp_note": "train (no holdout available), BEST FRACTAL",
    ...
}
```

**Finding**: The claimed JP scores (0.78-0.79) are explicitly marked as **"train (no holdout available)"** — they are **training metrics**, not holdout-validated jurist preference scores. Training scores are known to overestimate generalization performance.

**Actual holdout-validated JP** (from same file):
- `linear_metric_epoch4`: **0.6050** (best holdout)
- `mahalanobis_metric_epoch4`: 0.5850
- `hybrid_stabilized_epoch1`: 0.5150

**Semantic baseline 0.43**: Appears to derive from `baseline_1000` evaluation at resolution 3.0 (ratio=0.433) in `unified_results.json` — a **1,000-decision subset**, not 174k scale.

**Assessment**: The "JP 0.78 vs 0.43" claim compares **training scores at 1k scale** to a **baseline ratio at 1k scale** — not a fair holdout comparison at production scale. However, this limitation is **honestly documented in the source code** (`jp_note: "train (no holdout available)"`). The product lane correctly preserves negative results: dense embeddings FAIL jurist preference at all scales (JP 0.05-0.43), OOS JP ceiling ~0.53 < 0.7 factory target.
