## 5. Attack Surface Analysis

### 5.1 Selection-on-Test-Set Fix — Adversarial Check

**Attack**: Could the train-set selection still leak holdout information through indirect channels?

**Analysis**:
- The training pairs are created from train decisions only (line 386-540): `create_diversified_contrastive_pairs` takes `decisions_train` and `train_cp_embeddings` — no holdout data.
- The reference coarse labels are computed on train-only center_projected embeddings (line 248-257): `get_reference_coarse_labels_train_only(train_cp_embeddings)` — no holdout data.
- The contrastive pairs use branch/language/legal_area/chamber/outcome metadata from train decisions only.
- The training loop evaluates only train adversarial benchmarks for model selection.
- The holdout is evaluated exactly once after training completes.

**No indirect holdout leakage channels identified.** The fix is genuine.

### 5.2 Hierarchy-Loss Effect Magnitude

**Attack**: Is the +0.030 JP hierarchy effect real or noise?

**Analysis**:
- Both arms use identical code, seed, pairs, and data — the ONLY difference is `USE_HIERARCHY` flag.
- Both selected epoch 3 (same training dynamics up to that point).
- The +0.030 gap (0.535 vs 0.505) is larger than the +0.005-0.010 gap vs v10 baselines (which the report correctly calls noise).
- However, with only 200 holdout decisions, the jurist-preference proxy is inherently noisy. A +0.030 gap on 200 decisions corresponds to ~6 decisions — meaningful directionally but not statistically definitive.
- The report correctly states: "direction positive but magnitude is smaller than the selection-biased +0.055" and "both arms pass the jurist gate, so NOT load-bearing."

**Assessment**: The report's characterization is honest. The +0.030 is a real observed difference under controlled conditions, but the 200-decision proxy noise floor means it should be treated as indicative, not definitive.

### 5.3 Noise Floor Claim

**Attack**: Is the "within noise floor" characterization for the +0.005-0.010 gap vs v10 baselines justified?

**Analysis**:
- The jurist-preference proxy counts whether any of k=10 neighbors has the same branch but different language. On 200 decisions, a single decision's neighbor set changes by 1/200 ≈ 0.5%.
- The +0.005-0.010 gap corresponds to 1-2 decisions out of 200 — well within the expected variance of a stochastic neighbor-based metric.
- The report's "roughly tied" characterization is appropriate.

**Assessment**: Noise floor claim is justified.

### 5.4 Recurring Pathology Check

**Attack**: Was selection-on-holdout a recurring pathology?

**Analysis**:
- The selection-on-holdout defect was found once (cycle 33317369483), and this repair round fixes it.
- The v10 OOS metric learning used correct train-set selection (verified in prior audits).
- The v9 pre-trained metric learning had a different issue (data leakage from including holdout in training) — not the same pathology.
- This is the first and only instance of selection-on-holdout in the legal-distance lane.

**No recurring pathology.** The defect was a one-time methodological error, now corrected.
