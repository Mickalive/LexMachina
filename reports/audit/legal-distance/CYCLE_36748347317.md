1. This is explicitly a **slice evaluation** (years 2000–2014), not the full 174k corpus.
2. The control plane does not claim this passes adversarial gates.
3. The year-split computation is described as "IN PROGRESS" — this is an intermediate checkpoint.

**Verdict**: Not a defect for this cycle. The work honestly characterizes its scope.

## Findings

### Critical Finding: 174k Dense Embeddings (15-Year Slice) Do Not Pass Adversarial Gates

**Evidence**: All three representations (raw 768, CP 768, CP 64) fail both language dominance (< 0.85) and jurist pairwise preference (> 0.5) on the frozen harness v3.

**Implication**: The 174k dense embeddings pipeline has not yet produced a representation that clears the adversarial gates at scale. This is consistent with the control plane's "IN PROGRESS" status.

### Positive Signal: Center Projection Improves Jurist Preference 8–9×

**Evidence**: Raw JP=0.0315 → CP 768 JP=0.267 → CP 64 JP=0.2825.

**Implication**: Language-center projection removes substantial language artifact. The remaining gap to threshold (0.5) is ~0.22–0.23. Further work (metric learning, hybrid objectives, citation-role signals) may close this.

### Cluster Coherence Passes Only for CP 768 (Not CP 64)

**Evidence**: CP 768 passes cluster_coherence (branch_purity=0.739, lang_purity=0.840); CP 64 fails (branch_purity=0.681, lang_purity=0.830).

**Implication**: 64-dim PCA loses legal structure retained in 768-dim. The v6 finding that "ONLY 64-dim passes both gates WITH meaningful hierarchy" was on a 1,000-decision slice with metric learning; here, without metric learning, 768-dim CP outperforms 64-dim CP.

### Cross-Language Transfer Gap Narrows with Center Projection

**Evidence**: Transfer gap Raw=+0.047 → CP 768=+0.021 → CP 64=-0.008.

**Implication**: Center projection improves cross-lingual alignment, but zero-shot NMI remains low (~0.33–0.36 vs in-domain ~0.38–0.41).
