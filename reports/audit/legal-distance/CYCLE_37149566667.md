## 6. Critical Findings

### 6.1 Two-Mode Tradeoff is Structural, Not Engineering
The product requires **two distinct map modes**:
1. **Primary (TF-IDF citation hybrids)**: Jurist preference, branch clustering, legal neighbor retrieval
2. **Complementary (Dense embeddings)**: Citation heritage recovery, cross-lingual fact/holding alignment, linear hybrid bridge

No amount of scale or engineering on dense embeddings alone will close the jurist preference gap. The gap is fundamental: semantic embeddings optimize for cross-lingual semantic similarity; jurist preference optimizes for citation-graph legal relevance.

### 6.2 Minimal Scales Established
- **Citation heritage**: 21yr/137k (sufficient citation pairs from 2019+)
- **Section cross-lingual**: 1K sample (only scale tested; full corpus BLOCKED)
- **Linear hybrid complement**: 19yr/122k (first PASS both adversarial gates)

### 6.3 Data Blockers Are Upstream Dependencies
1. **No bger_ yearly corpus files** (2000-2019): Canonical corpus uses `bge_` IDs (published BGE, ~6,243 decisions); evaluation metadata uses `bger_` IDs (unpublished, 173,963 entries)
2. **No bge_ ↔ bger_ ID mapping**: Cross-reference does not exist
3. **Missing parquet for 2022-2026**: 29,520 decisions have no embedding checkpoints
4. **Section extraction not run at 174k scale**: Requires bger_ full-text access

These are **corpus lane** dependencies. Legal-distance lane correctly reports `BLOCKED_ON_DEPENDENCIES`.
