### 2. HNSW Artifact Fix — VALIDATED AND CORRECT

**Prior pathology:** HNSW on full 174k corpus masked representation differences in adversarial benchmarks (approximate NN introduced noise that equalized language dominance and jurist preference across representations).

**Fix implemented:** 
- Adversarial benchmarks (language dominance, jurist pairwise) now use **exact k-NN** on a **fixed stratified subsample** of 2,000 valid decisions (stratified by 4 branches → 500 per branch)
- Full-corpus scale benchmarks (temporal stability, hierarchy coherence, cluster coherence, cross-language retrieval, boilerplate resistance) continue to use HNSW on appropriate subsamples (15k–30k)

**Audit assessment:** The fix is methodologically correct. Exact k-NN on a representative subsample is the appropriate ground-truth for adversarial benchmarks where we need to measure true representation quality without ANN approximation noise. The subsample size (2,000) is adequate for estimating mean language dominance and jurist preference with low variance (std ~0.19–0.20 for LangDom). Fixed seed=42 ensures reproducibility.

**Potential concern noted:** Only 90,632 of 173,963 decisions (52%) have known branch labels and are eligible for the adversarial subsample. The remaining ~48% (unknown branch) are excluded from adversarial evaluation. This is a data limitation, not a methodological flaw, and is transparently reported.

## Issues Identified (Non-Blocking)

### Minor: Adversarial Subsample Language Balance
The adversarial subsample (n=2,000) is stratified by **branch only** (4 branches × 500), not by language. Since language dominance is one of the adversarial metrics, perfect language balance would be ideal. However, branch and language are not perfectly confounded in Swiss Federal Supreme Court data, and the subsample inherits the natural language distribution within each branch. This is a minor design choice, not a flaw. The exact k-NN methodology on this fixed subsample is still a major improvement over HNSW on full corpus.

### Minor: Valid Decisions Exclusion
~48% of decisions (unknown branch) excluded from adversarial evaluation. This is a data quality issue in the corpus lane, not an evaluation methodology issue. Transparently reported.

### Methodological Note: v17b Reference Mismatch
The formal generalization test compares 174k TF-IDF ratios to v17b reference ratios from different representations. The team correctly reports the formal test as FAIL and explains why. This is proper scientific practice — the test design is sound, the reference just isn't comparable. The substantive finding (5–10x gains) stands independently.
