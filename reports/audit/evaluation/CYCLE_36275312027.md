### Claim 3: Root Cause — 18.3% Metadata Coverage & Partial Corpus Center-Projection Artifact

**Evidence from data:**
- Total decisions in expanded evaluation: 12,570
- Valid decisions (with known branch metadata): 2,300
- **Metadata coverage: 2,300 / 12,570 = 18.3%** ✅ **MATCHES CLAIM**

**Technical analysis:**
- The `center_projected_768dim` embeddings are computed by centering multilingual-e5 embeddings on the **partial corpus centroid** (years 2000-2002 only), not the full 174k corpus centroid.
- Multilingual-e5 embeddings have strong inherent language clustering.
- When centering on a small, non-representative partial corpus, the centroid is biased toward the language distribution of that slice.
- The resulting centered embeddings retain dominant language structure because the "center" subtraction doesn't remove language variance — it just shifts it.
- At 18.3% metadata coverage, the adversarial benchmark's fixed stratified subsample (n=2000 from valid decisions) has very few decisions with known branch, making branch coherence measurement noisy and language dominance overwhelming.

**Comparison to 1,200-slice center_projected (which PASS adversarial):**
- The 1,200-slice evaluation used a **different, curated subset** with higher metadata density and likely different centering.
- Not comparable — the 1,200-slice was a controlled test; the 12,570 evaluation is an uncontrolled expansion of the legal-distance year-split output.

**Verification:** ✅ **ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS IS TECHNICALLY SOUND**. The 18.3% metadata coverage figure is arithmetically correct. The partial corpus center-projection artifact explanation is consistent with known properties of multilingual-e5 and centroid-based debiasing.
