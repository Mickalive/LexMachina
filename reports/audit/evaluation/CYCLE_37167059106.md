## Executive Summary

**The central claim — that v17b label normalization generalizes to 174k — is NOT SUPPORTED.**

While the 1.2k subset (`v17b_label_normalization_all_reps`) shows uniform improvements across all metrics (hierarchy +15-24%, zoom_fine +18-28%, legal_area +13-15%), the full 174k evaluation **fails** on the zoom_fine metric for 4 of 8 representations, with degradations of 11-17%.

The evaluation lane correctly reports this failure (`uniform_improvement_or_matching: false`), but the work product constitutes a **negative result** for the generalization hypothesis, not a successful validation.

## Detailed Findings

### 1. Core Result: Generalization Hypothesis Falsified

| Metric | 1.2k Subset (all_reps) | 174k Full Corpus | Generalization? |
|--------|------------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| hierarchy purity ratio | 1.15 – 1.24 (all ✅) | 1.00 (all representations) | ❌ No gain at scale |
| zoom_fine purity ratio | 1.18 – 1.28 (all ✅) | 0.84 – 0.99 (4/8 < 0.90) | ❌ **Regression at scale** |
| legal_area purity ratio | 1.13 – 1.15 (all ✅) | 0.9997 – 1.0024 (all ≈1.0) | ❌ No gain at scale |

**Representations with >10% zoom_fine degradation at 174k:**
- `cited_decisions_tfidf`: 0.8869
- `full_text_tfidf_light`: 0.8352
- `cited_decisions_tfidf_outcome_hybrid_0.5`: 0.8827
- `cited_decisions_tfidf_outcome_hybrid_0.7`: 0.8861

The `uniform_improvement_or_matching` flag correctly returns `false`. This is honest reporting of a negative result.

### 2. Suspicious Metric Behavior: Hierarchy Purity Constant at 0.4711

**Critical finding:** For ALL 8 representations, both raw and normalized, the `hierarchy_coherence.best_purity` is **exactly 0.4711** — identical to 4 decimal places.

This is statistically implausible for independent clustering runs on different embedding spaces. The value 0.4711 ≈ 81,920 / 173,963, suggesting the "purity" is actually measuring the majority-class baseline (the "unknown" legal_area with 82,770 decisions = 47.6% of corpus).

**Code inspection confirms the bug:** In `run_hierarchy_coherence()`, purity is computed on a 30k sample against 16 clusters, but the legal_area labels have 214 unique values. The dominant "unknown" class (47.6%) will dominate any 16-cluster KMeans assignment, yielding ~0.47 purity regardless of embedding quality.

**Impact:** The hierarchy_coherence benchmark is **measuring label imbalance, not embedding quality**. The claimed "1.0 ratio" (no change) is an artifact of a broken metric, not evidence of preservation.

### 3. Metric Mislabeling: "fine_purity" Is Actually NMI

In `run_zoom_coherence()`:
```python
fine_nmi = normalized_mutual_info_score(sample_labels, fine_labels)
return {'fine_purity': float(fine_nmi), ...}
```

The field `fine_purity` stores **NMI (normalized mutual information)**, not cluster purity. This mislabeling propagates to the output JSON where `zoom_coherence.fine_purity` is actually NMI.

**Impact:** The "purity ratio" for zoom_fine is actually an **NMI ratio**. The degradations (0.84–0.89) reflect NMI decline, not purity decline. While still a valid signal of degradation, the metric name is misleading.

### 4. Normalization Coverage Only ~49%

- Total decisions: 173,963
- Labels normalized: 85,819 (49.3%)
- Raw unique areas: 214 → Normalized unique areas: 164 (23% reduction)

The cross-lingual map covers 67 label triplets (de/fr/it), but many legal_area values are singletons, "unknown" (82,770 decisions), or already-canonical umbrella labels. The normalization only affects multilingual duplicates, not the long tail of rare/singleton labels.

### 5. Sampling Variance Risk

All three benchmarks use random subsampling:
- hierarchy_coherence: 30,000 samples
- zoom_coherence: 15,000 samples  
- legal_area_clustering: 15,000 samples

With frozen seed=42, results are reproducible, but **subsample choice affects absolute metric values**. The 174k results are not directly comparable to the 1.2k subset results (which used the full 1,148 decisions without sampling). The generalization comparison is therefore confounded by methodology differences.

### 6. Legal Area Clustering Metric Also Compromised

The `legal_area_clustering` benchmark clusters into `n_areas = len(unique_labels_in_sample)` clusters. With 214 raw areas (164 normalized) but only 15k samples, many areas have 0 or 1 samples in the subsample. The resulting NMI scores are very low (0.03–0.14), near noise floor. The ~1.0 purity ratios reflect both raw and normalized being equally uninformative at this sample rate.

## Verdict

This evaluation cycle **honestly reports a negative result**: the v17b label normalization does NOT generalize to 174k scale. The zoom_fine metric (NMI) degrades >10% for half the representations, hierarchy purity shows no gain (and is measured by a broken metric), and legal_area clustering shows no meaningful change.

The work is methodologically sound in its failure detection — the `uniform_improvement_or_matching: false` gate correctly triggers. However, two **code defects** (hierarchy purity bug, metric mislabeling) mean the reported metrics don't measure what they claim.

**No repair is needed for the evaluation result itself** (negative results are valid evidence). The code bugs should be fixed in the harness before any future use of these benchmarks.
