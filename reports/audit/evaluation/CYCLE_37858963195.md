## 4. Attack Surface Analysis

### 4.1 Threshold Lenience — Language Dominance (HIGH RISK)

**The language dominance threshold (0.85) is critically lenient.** All 8 representations achieve mean language dominance between 0.38–0.49, far below the 0.85 threshold. This means:
- A representation where 49% of neighbors share the same language **passes**
- The threshold permits up to 85% language homogeneity in neighborhoods
- This does not meaningfully test "language should not dominate neighbors"

**Evidence:** The max language dominance across representations reaches 0.95–1.0 (some decisions have 100% same-language neighbors), but the *mean* threshold masks this. A stricter threshold (e.g., 0.60 or percentile-based) would differentiate representations.

### 4.2 Citation Graph Coverage (HIGH RISK)

The citation heritage benchmark operates on a **severely sparse citation graph**:
- Total corpus: 173,963 decisions
- Decisions in citation graph: **174 (0.1%)**
- Resolved citations in corpus: 924
- Frozen pair pool: 1,020 positive + 1,020 negative pairs (but most unresolved)

This means the citation heritage benchmark evaluates on a **non-representative 0.1% subsample** of the corpus. Results cannot generalize to the full 174k scale. The `regeste_tfidf` anomaly (22 positive pairs) directly stems from this.

### 4.3 Subsample Representativeness (MEDIUM RISK)

The adversarial benchmarks use a stratified subsample of 2,000 decisions (1.15% of corpus) for exact k-NN. While stratified by branch × language, this may not capture:
- Rare legal areas
- Temporal distribution shifts
- Cross-chamber procedural variation

The HNSW artifact fix (exact k-NN on subsample) is methodologically sound but the subsample size is a frozen parameter that limits statistical power.

### 4.4 Jurist Pairwise Preference Simulation (MEDIUM RISK)

The jurist simulation assumes:
1. A jurist prefers same-branch/different-language over same-language/different-branch
2. If both available, jurist correctly chooses legal-relevant
3. If only language-artifact available, jurist is "forced wrong"

This is a **reasonable but simplified model**. It doesn't account for:
- Jurists using citation context, not just branch/language
- Cases where language alignment *is* legally relevant (e.g., language-specific statutes)
- The "neither available" case (22–186 decisions) where jurist has no viable neighbor

The `outcome_tfidf` failure (186 "neither available") suggests this representation creates semantic voids.

### 4.5 Data Leakage Check (LOW RISK)

No evidence of data leakage found:
- Frozen seed (42) used throughout
- Stratified subsampling uses fixed seed
- Citation pairs are pre-computed frozen pool (seed=42)
- No test-set decisions used in training embeddings (TF-IDF is unsupervised)

### 4.6 Boilerplate Confounding (LOW RISK — Not Tested)

The full evaluation harness includes boilerplate resistance testing, but it was not run in this cycle. TF-IDF representations are known to be susceptible to boilerplate (procedural language), so this gap matters for production readiness.

## 10. Conclusion

The evaluation correctly executes the frozen adversarial benchmarks on a stratified subsample and honestly reports results. The core finding — 7/8 TF-IDF representations pass both adversarial gates, `outcome_tfidf` fails — is **valid and reproducible**.

However, the evaluation has **significant methodological limitations**:
1. Language dominance threshold is too lenient to be meaningful
2. Citation heritage benchmark covers only 0.1% of corpus
3. v17b label normalization and full corpus benchmarks were not executed
4. No comparison to production baseline (center_projected_64dim)

These limitations do not invalidate the adversarial gate results but **do limit the claim ceiling**. The evaluation is suitable for **developmental comparison of TF-IDF variants** but not for **production gating at 174k scale**.

**Gate: PASS** — No critical defects requiring repair. The work is honestly represented with negative results preserved. Methodological issues are documented for future improvement but are not "same-cycle repairable" (they require human/architectural decisions on threshold tightening, citation graph expansion, and baseline inclusion).
