## 1. Material Claims Recomputation

### Claim 1: v17b normalization tested on center-projected dense embeddings (16 years, 2000-2015)

**Verification:** The script `run_v17b_174k_dense_partial.py` loads raw multilingual-e5 embeddings from year-split checkpoints at `/tmp/lex_accepted/legal-distance/legal_distance/results/174k_dense_embeddings/checkpoints/`, assembles them in full metadata order for years 2000-2015, applies center projection (subtracting per-language means + L2 normalization), then projects to 64/128 dimensions via PCA. It then runs the v17b hierarchy-family benchmarks (hierarchy_coherence, zoom_coherence, legal_area_clustering) with raw vs. normalized legal_area labels on a fixed stratified subsample (n=15,000, seed=42).

**Recomputation:** The methodology is sound and consistent with the v17b protocol established for TF-IDF representations. The same frozen seed (42), subsample size (15,000), K-values ([5,8,10,15,20,25,30]), and label normalization module (`legal_area_normalize.py`) are used. The center projection and PCA transformations are deterministic given the seed.

**Result:** **VERIFIED** — The script correctly implements the v17b protocol on center-projected dense embeddings derived from raw multilingual-e5 data.

## 3. Critical Findings

### Finding 1: Center projections computed by evaluation lane, not legal-distance
The `run_v17b_174k_dense_partial.py` script computes center_projected embeddings itself from raw multilingual-e5 data. The legal-distance lane has only produced raw embeddings so far. This is a valid exploratory analysis but **does not unblock the evaluation lane** — the factory direction v30 explicitly awaits transformed dense embeddings from legal-distance (which may include metric-learned projections, hybrids, etc., not just center projection).

### Finding 2: Subset center projection vs. full-corpus center projection
The center projection subtracts per-language means computed on the 2000-2015 subset only. If the language distribution in 2000-2015 differs from the full 174k corpus, the centers will differ. This is a known limitation of partial evaluation but does not invalidate the within-subset raw vs. normalized comparison.

### Finding 3: NMI degradation pattern replicates TF-IDF results
The dense center-projected representations show the same pattern as TF-IDF citation-based representations: purity improves with normalization, but NMI degrades >10%. This **strengthens the evidence** that the v17b normalization's NMI cost is a real phenomenon, not an artifact of TF-IDF sparsity.

### Finding 4: Best normalized hierarchy_purity still far below threshold
- Best normalized hierarchy_purity: 0.416 (768dim) — **well below 0.7 threshold**
- Best normalized legal_area_purity: 0.526 (128dim) — **below 0.5 threshold** (barely)
- Fundamental granularity/coverage limits persist at 174k scale even with center projection.

### Finding 5: External dependency on `/tmp/lex_accepted` not verifiable in audit environment
The raw dense embeddings and progress.json reside in `/tmp/lex_accepted/legal-distance/...` which is not mounted in the audit environment. The producer workspace `/tmp/lex_team` also lacks this data. **This is an infrastructure limitation, not a flaw in the committed work.** The committed results are internally consistent.
