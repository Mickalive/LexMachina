### 3.3 Boilerplate Resistance Metric — Validity Concern

**FINDING: Metric conflates chamber specialization with procedural boilerplate**

The boilerplate resistance metric defines:
- **Boilerplate neighbors (bad):** Same chamber, different legal_area
- **Legal neighbors (good):** Different chamber, same legal_area

**Problem:** In the Swiss Federal Supreme Court, chambers ARE specialized by legal area:
- I. & II. Zivilrechtliche Abteilung → both `zivilrecht` (Civil Law)
- I. & II. Strafrechtliche Abteilung → both `strafrecht` (Criminal Law)
- I.–IV. Öffentlich-rechtliche Abteilung → all `oeffentliches_recht` (Public Law)

Thus "same chamber, different legal_area" is **rare or impossible** at the branch level. The `legal_area` field (e.g., "Contract Law", "Family Law") is more fine-grained than branch, but metadata coverage/sparsity is unknown.

**Observed result:** ALL representations show ~94-96% boilerplate neighbor rate vs ~4-5% legal neighbor rate. This likely reflects:
1. Chamber-level procedural/style similarity dominating embeddings (known issue)
2. `legal_area` sparsity/noise making "different chamber, same legal_area" pairs scarce
3. The metric measuring **chamber coherence** rather than procedural boilerplate per se

**Audit judgment:** The negative result is **real and important** — embeddings capture chamber-level patterns. But the metric's label "boilerplate resistance" overclaims. The report honestly states "This is a fundamental property of the current embedding approaches and Swiss Federal Supreme Court corpus structure." This honest framing mitigates the metric validity concern.

### 3.4 Jurivoc Proxy — Known Limitation

The harness uses:
- **Level 0 (4 branches):** Public, Civil, Criminal, Social Security → **Valid**
- **Level 1 (16 legal_areas):** From metadata `legal_area` field → **Proxy, coverage unknown**

The report correctly labels this "Jurivoc proxy" and notes "Since we don't have full Jurivoc annotations for all 1200 decisions, we use branch + legal_area as proxy." This transparency is appropriate.
