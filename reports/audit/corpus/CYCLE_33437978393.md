## Critical Finding: Field Coverage Methodology Mismatch

The cycle report (v25_cycle_33437978393_report.md) presents a **field coverage table** labeled "Field Coverage (2024 sample, n=1000, seed=42)":

| Field | Report (2024-only) | Ground Truth (Cross-year, validation_report_v14.json) |
|-------|-------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| full_text | 100.0% | 100.0% |
| cited_decisions | **58.2%** | **52.6%** |
| legal_area | **58.2%** | **52.6%** |
| outcome | **56.8%** | **50.5%** |
| regeste | **41.8%** | **47.4%** |
| bge_reference | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| cited_laws | 0.0% | 0.0% |

**Assessment**: The report accurately describes its methodology ("2024 sample") but **presents 2024-only numbers alongside full-corpus claims without clarifying they are not representative**. The 2024 data has systematically different characteristics:
- Higher cited_decisions/legal_area coverage (58.2% vs 52.6%)
- Higher outcome coverage (56.8% vs 50.5%)
- Lower regeste coverage (41.8% vs 47.4%)

The **state file correctly reports cross-year ground truth** from `validation_report_v14.json` (sample_size=1000, seed=42, drawn from all 174,113 decisions). This is the authoritative field coverage.

### Test Sampling Bias (Additional Finding)

The v14 test suite contains **two different sampling methodologies** for field coverage tests:

1. **`test_field_coverage_restege_above_40pct`** — Loads ALL records from all year files, samples 1000 (matches validation methodology) → **47.4% PASS** (matches ground truth)

2. **`test_field_coverage_cited_decisions_above_50pct`** and **`test_field_coverage_outcome_above_45pct`** — Load only 500 records per year file (max ~18,500 records), then sample 1000 → **99.3% and 100% PASS** (severely inflated, NOT representative)

The test thresholds (≥40%, ≥50%, ≥45%) are set low enough that both methodologies pass, but the 500-per-year methodology produces **misleadingly high coverage numbers** that do not reflect the full corpus. This is a **test design defect** that could mask regressions.
